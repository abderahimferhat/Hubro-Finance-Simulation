# Hubro Finance Simulation — AeroSix Innovations

## Overview

This project documents my financial analysis and strategic decision-making experience in the **Hubro Business Simulation**, where I managed the financial performance of **AeroSix Innovations**, a simulated jetpack manufacturing company.

The project applies core principles of **managerial finance, financial statement analysis, cash-flow management, capital structure, investment analysis, forecasting, and operational decision-making** to a dynamic business environment.

Rather than analyzing a company solely from historical financial statements, the simulation required financial decisions to be evaluated based on their expected impact on **profitability, liquidity, working capital, financing requirements, and long-term firm value**.

---

AeroSix Innovations operates in the jetpack market and competes across multiple product categories, including:

## Cash Flow Analysis

Cash-flow management was a major component of the simulation because investment and financing decisions could create significant liquidity requirements.

| Cash Flow Metric                   |        Amount |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------: |
| Net Cash from Operating Activities |       $63,700 |
| Net Cash from Investing Activities | $(183,333.34) |
| Net Cash from Financing Activities |      $160,000 |
| Net Increase in Cash               |    $40,366.66 |
| Beginning Cash                     |    $23,451.50 |
| Ending Cash                        |    $63,818.16 |

The company generated **$63,700 of operating cash flow**, indicating that its core operations were producing positive cash.

At the same time, investing activities consumed approximately **$183,333**, primarily through:

* $100,000 investment in property, plant and equipment

This illustrates an important managerial-finance principle: a company can be profitable while simultaneously requiring external financing because capital expenditures and strategic investments create cash requirements before their expected returns are realized.

## Key Financial Insights

Several important financial-management principles were demonstrated through the simulation:

### Profitability does not equal liquidity

Although AeroSix Innovations generated positive net income and operating cash flow, substantial investment expenditures created a significant financing requirement.

### Growth requires capital

Investment in production capacity and R&D can reduce short-term cash balances while creating potential long-term growth opportunities.

### Inventory must be aligned with demand

Finished-goods inventory represented a substantial portion of current assets. Producing too aggressively could tie up capital and increase inventory risk, while underproduction could result in missed sales opportunities.

### Debt can accelerate growth but increases financial risk

The company used $160,000 in long-term debt financing while also carrying $100,000 in current loan obligations. Debt allowed the company to fund investments but increased future financial commitments.

### Forecasting supports capital allocation

Market and demand forecasts provided a basis for determining whether additional production, investment, or financing would be economically justified.
